# How to play Triage RUSH!

(note: this 'how to play' note was written by claude, summarizing the rules)


You are the triage nurse at the front of a busy emergency department.

Patients arrive in the waiting room. Your job is to read each one and send
them to the right place — **not** to work out what is wrong with them.

> **This is TRIAGE, not DIAGNOSIS.**
>
> Triage asks *how sick is this person, and how soon do they need to be seen?*
> You are sorting, not treating.

---

## A shift, step by step

**1. Tap a waiting patient** on the left rail. They walk to the centre.

**2. Read them.** The centre panel shows their name, age, chief complaint,
something they say in their own words, and six vital signs. Tap the panel to
open the full **chart** — the triage note, any red flags, and the clinical
detail.

The clock keeps running while you read. Studying a patient properly costs you
time, and that is the whole game.

**3. Tap a door** on the right rail to send them there. There are seven:

| Door | Means |
|---|---|
| **ESI 1 — Resuscitation** | Dying now. Needs the team this minute. |
| **ESI 2 — Emergent** | High risk. Cannot safely wait. |
| **ESI 3 — Urgent** | Needs care and several resources, but is stable. |
| **ESI 4 — Less urgent** | One resource, roughly. |
| **ESI 5 — Non-urgent** | Minor. Could safely wait a long time. |
| **Psych** | Behavioural health. |
| **Discharge** | Does not need the department at all. |

**4. You are told straight away** whether that was right.

Changed your mind? **Tap the open door to recall** that patient and place them
again. Only your most recent placement counts.

**5. The shift ends** when the clock runs out, or when you end it early. The
**Shift Report** gives you your score, how many patients you saw, and every
chart to look back over at your leisure.

---

## Scoring

| Result | Points |
|---|---|
| Correct room | **+100** |
| Close | **+50** |
| Wrong | **−50** |

**Patients still waiting cost you nothing.** A full waiting room is the
premise of the game, not a failure on your part. You are measured on the
decisions you actually made.

### Two difficulties

- **Forgiving** — one step away from the correct room still earns partial
  credit.
- **Strict** — only the correct room scores at all.

---

## Two modes

| | |
|---|---|
| **Triage!** | The steady one. Five patients waiting, refilled as you take them. Five or ten minutes. |
| **Triage RUSH!** | The pressure one. Patients keep arriving, faster and faster, up to ten deep. Sixty or one hundred and twenty seconds. |

Start with **Triage!** at five minutes on Forgiving. Move to RUSH when reading
a patient stops feeling slow.

---

## Controls

Everything is a tap. There is nothing to type, and no keyboard ever appears.

| Where | What it does |
|---|---|
| Waiting rail | Tap a patient to select them — or to swap for the one you are holding |
| Patient panel | Tap to open the chart; tap the photo to enlarge it |
| Room rail | Tap a door to place the patient; tap an open door to recall them |
| Header | The note icon mutes and unmutes everything |
| Footer | Quit the shift, or end it early and go to the report |

On the entrance screen, the two **sidewalk boards** are your settings — the
left one is your name, the right one the game and sound options. Nothing
labels them as tappable. Try them.

The game also works with a keyboard, and respects your system's
reduced-motion setting.

---

## Sound

Three settings, on the game options board:

- **GLOBAL SOUND** — the master switch. Off silences everything.
- **GAME SOUNDS** — off / lo / hi. Arrivals, placements, the ticking clock,
  the ten-second beats, and the bell at the end of a shift. All of it is
  synthesised in the browser as you play; there are no sound files.
- **MUSIC** — off / lo / hi. Background music, deliberately quiet.

Both levels preview as you tap them, so you can hear what you are choosing
before you accept it.

**The MUSIC row is not always there.** There is background music in this game,
and your own initials are what find it. That is all this file is going to say.
