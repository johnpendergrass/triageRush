### Shift Review appearance — mockups, not code

John asked for the Shift Review to look more like a real medical review, and
to be more legible, WITHOUT altering the game wholesale. Result:
`_mockups/shift-review-mockups.html`, a standalone harness with six variants:

- **0 CURRENT** — today's build verbatim, as the thing to beat.
- **A FULL** — "FORM ED-7", an attending's evaluation of a resident. Form
  number and revision, ruled field blanks, the scoring table as a rubric, a
  marked BELOW/MEETS/EXCEEDS row, a comments box written from the player's
  actual pattern, a signature, coffee ring and rubber stamp.
- **A2 LITE** — the same form with each idea on one line. **John's preferred
  direction**, subject to seeing it on a real phone.
- **A3 MINIMAL** — score, grade, one sentence, signature.
- **B LEGIBLE** — today's content with the legibility problems fixed.
- **C COMPETENCY** — a milestones-style card with rated bars.

Verified programmatically: all six fit the 9:16 shell with NO scrolling at
both desktop and 320px-phone widths.

Legibility findings worth keeping: the secondary ink was `#6d7b81` (too
light on cream — now `#4b585e`), and **letter-spacing of 0.22em on the small
caps labels was hurting readability more than the font size was.**

### Two things John rejected outright, with reasons

- **"FOR LEVEL OF TRAINING" ratings.** John: "we are not medically trained to
  really figure that out are we?" Correct — the label claimed knowledge we do
  not have. Removed. The grade now makes no clinical claim and PRINTS ITS OWN
  BASIS ("55% correct placement") beneath itself.
- **Faulting the player for a full waiting room.** John: the game is premised
  on a full room and the player cannot empty it. A draft had the grade
  DEMOTING for patients left waiting. Removed, along with the scolding
  comment line and a throughput meter that measured
  `seen / (seen + waiting)`. Pace (patients per minute) replaced it, and is
  mentioned only when it is a credit.

  **This objection is what produced doc 10.** The reason the room could never
  be emptied is that the game had no patient limit.

### The finite patient pool design (doc 10)

Designed conversationally with John across the rest of the session and
recorded rule by rule. Headline: every timed shift gets a finite pool, so the
game gains a WIN STATE and the score gains a CEILING (`pool x 100`) — which
is what unblocks the Shift Review grade, since a percentage of the achievable
score needs no invented threshold.

Four profiles: RUSH 60s / pool 12, RUSH 120s / pool 20, TRIAGE 300s / pool 30,
and an untimed SANDBOX that REPLACES TRIAGE 600s. Full rules, per-profile
detail, and the constant table are in doc 10.

## The single most important caveat

**Every pool size is a guess, and no AI-produced figure is evidence.** The
patient counts used in the Shift Review mockups (11 seen in 5:00, 5 in 2:00)
were invented for layout. Calibrating 12 / 20 / 30 needs John to play a 60s
RUSH, a 120s RUSH and a 300s TRIAGE and report how many patients he actually
placed. That one input sets the pools AND the review's grade bands.

## Open decisions carried forward (all in doc 10 section 6)

1. Pool calibration (above).
2. **The last-patient recall window** — the shift currently ends the moment
   the final patient is assigned, which makes that one patient uniquely
   unfixable. Needs a ruling.
3. **Sandbox ledger keying** — the deck reshuffles on exhaustion, but
   `ledger.byPatientId` would MERGE a repeated patient into one record,
   miscounting the score. Provably sandbox-only, so "cap sandbox at 160" is a
   real alternative to an encounter-keyed ledger rework.
4. Keep or suppress the blocked-arrival shake in RUSH.
5. Should the waiting panel shrink below five during the final drain?
6. Optional: composed pools (fixed acuity recipe) so shifts are comparable.
