## What happened this session

### Phases 7 and 8 accepted

John played the build: "acceptable". Closes an item outstanding across four
handoffs — Phase 7 had never had a play-through verdict.

### A patient-limit redesign was designed in full, then ABANDONED

Most of the middle of the session went into a finite-patient-pool design (a
win state, a score ceiling, an untimed sandbox). John then decided against it
and to continue with the game as originally conceived. Nothing was built.
Both files are in `docs/archive/design-notes/` marked ABANDONED.
**Do not re-propose it.**

One consequence survives and matters: **the score has no ceiling**, so a
BELOW / MEETS / EXCEEDS grade can never be "a percentage of what was
achievable". Variant B carries no grade at all, which sidesteps it.

### The Shift Review's presentation: variant B

Six mockup treatments were built and reviewed on John's iPhone. He chose
**B ("LEGIBLE")** — today's content and structure with the legibility
problems fixed — over the FORM ED-7 supervisor-evaluation motif. A/A2/A3/C
were removed from the harness afterwards; they are in commit `2b925bc`.

The legibility findings are the substance of the variant and worth reusing on
any cream-paper surface: secondary ink `#6d7b81` is too light (use
`#4b585e`), and **0.22em letter-spacing on small-caps labels hurt readability
more than the font size did** — ~0.10em reads far better.

### Two permanent rulings from John

- **Never claim performance "for level of training".** John: "we are not
  medically trained to really figure that out are we?" Any grade must print
  its own basis and make no clinical claim.
- **Never fault the player for a full waiting room.** The game is premised on
  a room the player cannot empty. This produced approved item 1, dropping the
  left-waiting penalty from scoring entirely.

### A lost authoring rule, recovered

John remembered a rule about when Psych may be the correct room, could not
find it, and asked whether he was misremembering. It was real — decided
around 2026-07-25 and never promoted out of the archive, which is why it got
misremembered twice (once by John, once by Claude inventing a different rule
from the data). **It is now written into doc 5** as an explicit authoring
rule: dangerous psychiatric presentations are ESI 1-2 medical and never
Psych; Psych is correct only for medically stable ESI 4-5 (in practice 4);
Discharge only at ESI 5.

**Lesson worth keeping:** archive content cannot override the numbered docs,
so a rule that lives only in archive is a rule that will be misremembered.

## Correction recorded, so it is not repeated

Claude asserted "Psych is always ESI-4" as though it were a design rule. It
was an artifact of reading the 5 existing psych records. The direction rule
built on it (psych->4, discharge->5) was fragile and was replaced by the
room-rank ladder in doc 10 item 4, which never consults `correctEsi` and
therefore cannot break when a patient is authored differently.
